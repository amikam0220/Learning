# 1. Objective

To measure and calculate the focal distance of thin lenses including convex and concave and to master the different methods to measure the focal length.
# 2. Principle 

## 2.1 Converging lens

### 2.1.1 Plane Mirror Method

Utilize the light path is reversible. Thus we can arrange a plane mirror behind the lens and adjust the illuminated objects until the refracted rays are normal to the plane mirror. 
### 2.1.2 Lens Formula Method

$$\frac{1}{u}+\frac{1}{v}=\frac{1}{f}$$
where $u$ is the distance from the object to the lens, $v$ is the distance from the image to the lens, and $f$ is the focal length of the lens. So we can measure several value of $u,v$ to calculate the value of $f$.

### 2.1.3 Two-Position Method

Fix the object and screen and change the position of the lens until it processes another sharp image.
$$f= \frac{D^{2}-l^{2}}{4D}$$
where $D$ is the distance from the object to the screen, $l$ is the distance between two positions of the lens.
Note that the real image can not be obtained if the object is inside the principal focus or the distance between the object and the screen is less than $4f$. Thus in the method the separation of the object and screen must exceed $4f$.

## 2.2 Diverging Lens

## 2.2.1 Auxiliary Converging Lens Method

Since the diverging lens normally forms a virtual image, which cannot be located by a screen and is not easily found by pin or no parallax method, we need the help of a converging lens. 

Initially the diverging lens is absent and the image of the converging lens is located and notes its position, called $P_{1}$. Then place the diverging lens between the converging lens and $P_{1}$, then the diverging lens forms a real image of $P_{1}$ at $P_{2}$, thus $u$ is the distance between the diverging lens and $P_{1}$ and $v$ is the distance between the diverging lens and $P_{2}$. Then we can calculate the $f$ by upper formula.
# 3. Procedures

### 3.1 General Preparation for the Experiment

1. Check equipment: Ensure optical bench scales are clear, lenses and mirror are clean, and the light source works;
2. Coaxial adjustment: Align the centers of the object, lens, and screen with the optical bench’s principal axis;
3. Environment: Turn off nearby strong light to avoid interference.

### 3.2 Measurement of the Focal Length of the Convex Lens

#### 3.2.1 Plane Mirror Method

1. Arrange the equipment: Fix the convex lens at the middle position of the optical bench (denoted as $x_{1}$​), place the plane mirror behind the convex lens with its surface perpendicular to the principal axis of the optical bench, and place the illuminated object on the optical bench in front of the convex lens (denoted as $x_{2}$).
2. Move the illuminated object slowly, and at the same time, observe from the side of the object. Move your eyes left and right to check for "parallax": if there is no relative movement between the object and its image, the object and the image coincide.
3. Record $x_{2}$​ and $x_{1}$​ at this time, and calculate $f=$∣$x_{2}$​−$x_{1}$​∣. Repeat the measurement 3 times and take the average value as f obtained by this method.

#### 3.2.2 Lens Formula Method

1. Arrange the equipment: Place the illuminated object, convex lens, and screen on the optical bench in sequence, ensuring they are coaxial.
2. Move the illuminated object to the front of the convex lens (u>f), record the position of the object (x1​) and the position of the convex lens (x2​), then u=∣x2​−x1​∣.
3. Move the screen until a clear real image appears, record the position of the screen (x3​), then v=∣x3​−x2​∣.
4. Substitute the values into the formula f1​=u1​+v1​ to calculate f, and fill x1​, x2​, x3​, u, v, and f into Table 1.
5. Change the position of the illuminated object, repeat Steps 2-4, measure 5 sets of data in total, and calculate the average value of f.
6. Plot the v1​−u1​ graph: Take u1​ as the horizontal axis and v1​ as the vertical axis, mark the 5 sets of data points, draw a best-fit straight line, read the intercept, and calculate f.

#### 3.2.3 Two-Position Method

1. Arrange the equipment: Fix the illuminated object (at position x1​) and the screen (at position x4​), and calculate the distance between the object and the screen D=∣x4​−x1​∣ (ensure D>4f).
2. Place the convex lens between the object and the screen, move the lens slowly. When the first clear magnified image appears on the screen, record the position of the lens (x2​).
3. Continue moving the lens toward the screen. When the second clear diminished image appears on the screen, record the position of the lens (x3​), and calculate the distance between the two positions of the lens l=∣x3​−x2​∣.
4. Substitute the values into the formula $f=\frac{D^{2}-l^{2}}{4D}$​ to calculate f, and fill x1​, x2​, x3​, x4​, D, l, and f into Table 2.
5. Change the fixed positions of the object and the screen (i.e., change D), repeat Steps 1-4, measure 5 sets of data in total, and calculate the average value of f.

### 3.3 Measurement of the Focal Length of the Concave Lens (Auxiliary Converging Lens Method)

1. Arrange the equipment: Place the illuminated object, converging lens L1​ and screen on the optical bench in sequence, and adjust them to be coaxial. Move the screen until a clear real image P1​ appears, and record the position of P1​ (x2​) and the position of L1​.
2. Place the concave lens L2​ between L1​ and P1​, and fix the position of L2​ (x1). At this time, P1​ is the "virtual object" of L2​, and calculate the object distance u=∣x2−x1​∣.
3. Move the screen until a clear real image P2​ appears, record the position of P2​ (x3), and calculate the image distance v=∣x3−x1​∣.
4. Substitute the values into the formula f1​=u1​+v1​ to calculate f (the focal length f of the concave lens is negative), and fill x1, x2, x3​, u, v, and f into Table 3.
5. Change the position of L2​ between L1​ and P1​, repeat Steps 2-4, measure 3 sets of data in total, and calculate the average value of f.

# 5. Pre-Questions 

The other methods to measure the focal length of converging or diverging lens:
1. **Convex lens: Sunlight focusing method**: Face the lens at sunlight, move the screen to get the smallest bright spot (focal point). Measure spot-lens distance (focal length). Simple but error-prone (sunlight intensity, spot clarity).
2. **Concave lens: Parallax method (with auxiliary pin)**: Place an object on one side of the concave lens, an auxiliary pin on the other. Adjust the pin until it has no parallax with the concave lens’s virtual image. Record positions, calculate f.
# 6. ~~Questions~~

- What should be prepared before you measuring the focal length
	1. **Equipment**: Thin convex/concave lens, plane mirror, illuminated object, screen, optical bench, pins, ruler, lens tissue. Check: No optical bench jamming, no lens scratches/stains, uniform light source.
	2. **Conditions**: Align equipment centers with the optical bench axis; turn off nearby strong light; roughly estimate convex lens f (sunlight method) to ensure D>4f (two-position method).
- which method is more accurate and why.
	The **Two-Position Method** is more accurate, for the following reasons:
	1. Fewer errors: Only measure D (object-screen distance) and l (lens spacing), no separate u/v measurement (avoids position judgment errors).
	2. Systematic error offset: Optical bench scale errors affect D and l equally, offsetting in the formula f=4DD2−l2​.
	3. Multiple measurements: Average of 5 sets reduces accidental errors.
	  Other methods are less accurate: Plane mirror method is affected by mirror flatness and image-object coincidence judgment; lens formula method accumulates u/v measurement errors; auxiliary converging lens method relies on convex lens accuracy and virtual object position judgment.
