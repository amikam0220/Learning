# Capacitors and Capacitance

A capacitor is a device that ==stores electrical charge and energy==, converting other forms of energy into electrical energy.

Two-conductor capacitor is composed of two conductors/electrodes separated by a certain medium or vacuum. After charging, two conductors ==carry equal amount of opposite charges==.

And Capacitance:
$$
C=\frac{Q}{V}
$$

It is definition. Capacitance is ==independent of $Q$ and $V$==. $Q$ is the amount of charge on a ==single conductor==.(SI unit: $F$)

# Calculating the Capacitance

- Assume a charge $q$ on the plates
- Finding the electric field $\vec{E}$ by Gauss's Law
- Calculate the potential difference $V$ by $V=Ed$(uniform) or $V=\int Edr$
- Calculating $C$ from $C = \frac{q}{V}$.

# Capacitors in Series and Parallel

## Capacitors in Parallel

$$
\begin{align}
 & Q=Q_{1}+Q_{2}+\cdots \\
 & V=V_{1}=V_{2}=\cdots \\
 & C_{eq}=C_{1}+C_{2}+\cdots = \sum_{j=1}^{n} C_{j}
\end{align}
$$

## Capacitors in Series

$$
\begin{align}
 & Q=Q_{1}=Q_{2}=\cdots \\
 & V=V_{1}+V_{2}+\cdots \\
 & \frac{1}{C_{eq}}=\frac{1}{C_{1}}+\frac{1}{C_{2}}+\cdots
\end{align}
$$

# Energy Stored in An Electric-Field

During charging process, external forces do work and converting other forms of energy into electrical energy. Is is ==the energy of electric field==.

Energy Storage in Capacitors:
$$
U=\frac{1}{2}CV^{2}=\frac{1}{2} \frac{Q^{2}}{C}=\frac{1}{2}QV
$$

## Energy Density of Electric Field

$$
\begin{align}
 & V=Ed \\
 & E=\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_{0}} \\
 & U=\frac{1}{2}QV=\frac{1}{2}\sigma AEd=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{0}E^{2}Ad \\
\end{align}
$$

Thus, the energy density is 
$$
u=\frac{U}{Ad}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{0}E^{2}
$$

# Capacitor with A Dielectric

Dielectric are ==insulating materials==. The functions of dielectric:
- Bring two conductors of a capacitor very close together without them touching. ==Increases== the ==capacitance== of capacitor.
- Limit the potential difference to certain value $V_{\mathrm{max}}$, called ==breakdown potential==.

Dielectric constant:
$$
\kappa=\frac{C}{C_{0}}=\frac{\epsilon}{\epsilon_{0}}
$$
where $C$ and $\epsilon$ is the ==capacitance== and ==permittivity== of capacitors containing dielectric. $C_{0}$ is the capacitance of vacuum.

## Polarization of Dielectric

The net bound ==charge is generated inside and on the surface of the dielectric== redistributed by an electric field. The charge is called ==polarization charge(induced charge)== and this phenomenon is called ==dielectric polarization==.
![[polarization of dielectric.png]]
The ==electric field generated by the polarization charge== is ==opposite in direction to original electric field==.

The density of polarization charge and net polarization charge:
$$
\begin{align}
 & \sigma_{i}=-\sigma\left( 1- \frac{1}{\kappa} \right) \\
 & Q_{i}=-Q\left( 1 - \frac{1}{\kappa} \right)
\end{align}
$$

Dielectric breakdown: Under a sufficiently strong electric field, electrons are pulled out and collide with other molecules, releasing even more electrons. This instantaneous avalanche of electrons ==creates a spark/arc discharge==, causing a ==short circuit phenomenon==.
