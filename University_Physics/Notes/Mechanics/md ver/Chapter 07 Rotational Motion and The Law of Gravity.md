# The Radian

A unit of angular measure. Learned before.

# Angular Displacement

Axis of rotation is the center of the disk. For a ==fixed reference line==, the angle $\theta$ is the angular displacement during time $t$.

# Rigid Body

- ==Every point== on the object undergoes circular motion about the point O.
- ==All parts== of the object of the body rotate through the ==same angle during the same time==.
That is, ==each part== of the body is ==fixed== in position relative to all ==other parts== of the body. And this object is called ==rigid body==.

# Average Angular Speed

The ratio of the angular displacement to the time interval
$$
\omega_{av}=\frac{\Delta\theta}{\Delta t}
$$
# Average Angular Acceleration

The ratio of the angular speed to the time interval
$$
\alpha_{av}=\frac{\Delta\omega}{\Delta t}
$$

For a rigid body, every point has the same angular speed and acceleration.

# Analogies Between Linear and Rotational Motion

![[Linear and rotational motion.png]]
relationship between angular and linear quantities:
- Displacements:$s=\theta r$
- Speeds:$v_{t}=\omega r$
- Acceleration:$a_{t}=\alpha r$
For a rotating object, it has the same angular motion but not have the same linear motion.

# Centripetal Acceleration

The acceleration is directed ==toward the center== of the circle of motion. The magnitude of the centripetal acceleration is 
$$
a_{c} = \frac{v^{2}}{r}=\omega^{2}r
$$

And Newton's Second Law says the centripetal acceleration is accompanied by a force.
$$
F_{c}=ma_{c}
$$
The force stands for ==any force== keeps an object following a circular path. it is a ==fictitious force==.

# Total Acceleration

The tangential components of the acceleration is due to changing speed. The centripetal component of the acceleration is due to changing direction. And total acceleration is 
$$
a=\sqrt{ a_{t}^{2}+a_{c}^{2} }
$$

# Vector Nature of Angular Quantities

Angular displacement, velocity and acceleration are all ==vector quantities==. But the direction is defined by using ==right hand rule==.

# Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation

The force between any two particles.
$$
F=G \frac{m_{1}m_{2}}{r^{2}}
$$
It is an example of an ==inverse square law==. $G$ is a constant of universal gravitational, determined experimentally by Henry Cavendish.

# Gravitational Potential Energy

For object ==high above== the earth's surface, the gravitational potential energy is 
$$
PE=-G \frac{M_{E}m}{r}
$$
and zero reference level is infinitely far from the earth. $r=R_{E}+h$.
Near the earth's surface, we have 
$$
g=G \frac{M_{E}}{r^{2}}
$$
and thus 
$$
PE=mgh
$$

# Cosmic Speed

- First cosmic velocity: The speed for an object to into the space. The least speed for a object orbits the planet.
	$$G \frac{mm_{E}}{R^{2}}=\frac{mv_{1}^{2}}{R}=mg$$
	Thus $$v_{1}=\sqrt{ gR }=\sqrt{ \frac{Gm_{E}}{R} }$$
- Second cosmic velocity: The escape speed is the speed needed for an object to soar off into space and never return
	$$\frac{1}{2}mv^{2}-G \frac{mm_{E}}{r}=0$$
	thus $$v=\sqrt{ \frac{2Gm_{E}}{r} }=\sqrt{ 2gR }$$

# Kepler's Laws

1. All planets move in ==elliptical orbits== with the ==Sun at one of the focal points==.
2. A line drawn from the Sun to any planet sweeps out ==equal areas in equal time intervals==.
3. The square of the orbital period of any planet is proportional to cube of the average distance from the Sun to the planet. $T^{2}=Kr^{3}$.
