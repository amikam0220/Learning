# Torque

$\tau$, torque, is the ==tendency== of a force to rotate an object about some axis.
$$
\vec{\tau}=\vec{r}\times \vec{F}
$$
where $\vec{r}$ is the position vector, $\vec{F}$ is the force.
The direction of $\tau$ obeys ==right hand rule==.

# Equilibrium

To ensure mechanical equilibrium, we should ensure ==both translational equilibrium and rotational equilibrium==.
- Translation equilibrium:
	$$\sum \vec{F}=0$$
- Rotational Equilibrium:
	$$\sum \vec{\tau}=0$$

# Center of Gravity

The coordinates of the center of gravity can be found from ==the sum of the torque== acting on the individual particles being set ==equal to the torque produced by the weight of the object==.

The center of gravity of a homogenous. symmetric body must lie on the ==axis of symmetry==. And the center of gravity of such an object is the ==geometric center== of the object.

# Torque and Angular Acceleration

The angular acceleration is directly proportional to the net torque.
$$
\sum \tau=I\alpha
$$
Which is similar to $\sum F=ma$
Thus it is ==Newton's second Law for a rotating object==.
# Moment of Inertia

The mass analog is called the ==moment of inertia==, $I$.
$$
I=\sum mr^{2}
$$
or 
$$
I=\int r^{2} \, dm 
$$
Differences between moment of inertia and mass: The moment of inertia depends on the quantity of matter and its ==distribution== in the rigid object, and also depends on the ==location of the axis of rotation==.

# Rotational Kinetic Energy

An object rotating about some axis with an angular speed $\omega$ has rotational kinetic energy 
$$
KE_{r}=\frac{1}{2}I\omega^{2}
$$
# Total Energy of a System

Conservation of Mechanical Energy
$$
(KE_{t}+KE_{r}+PE_{g})_{i}=(KE_{t}+KE_{r}+PE_{g})_{f}
$$
other potential energies of any other conservative forces can be added.

# Work-Energy in a Rotating System

For nonconservative force, the work-energy theorem:
$$
W_{nc}=\Delta KE_{t}+\Delta KE_{R}+\Delta PE
$$

# Angular Momentum

Angular momentum is defined as 
$$
\vec{L}=I\vec{\omega}=m(\vec{r}\times \vec{v})
$$
and 
$$
\sum \vec{\tau}=\frac{\Delta \vec{L}}{\Delta t}
$$
It is similar to the relationship between force and momentum in a linear system.

If the net torque is zero, the angular momentum remains constant.
That is, when $\sum \vec{\tau}=0$, $\vec{L_{i}}=\vec{L_{f}}$ or $I_{i}\vec{\omega_{i}}=I_{f}\vec{\omega_{f}}$.
