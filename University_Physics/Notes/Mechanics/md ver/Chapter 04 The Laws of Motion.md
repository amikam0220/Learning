# Classical Mechanics

Describes the relationship between the motion of objects and the ==forces== acting on them.
Do not apply:
- Very tiny objects(smaller than atomic sizes)
- Objects moving near the speed of light

# Forces

## Types

- Strong nuclear force
- Electromagnetic force
- Weak nuclear force
- Gravity

All ==field forces==, listed in order of decreasing strength. Only ==gravity and electromagnetism== is in general physics.

# Newton's First Law

An object moves with a velocity that is constant in magnitude and direction, unless acted on by a nonzero net force.

==Inertia== is the ==tendency== of an object to continue in its original motion, relative to mass. Scalar quantity.

# Newton's Second Law

The acceleration is directly ==proportional to the net force== acting on it and ==inversely proportional to its mass==.
$$
\vec{a}=\frac{\sum\vec{F}}{m}
$$
or 
$$
\sum \vec{F}=m\vec{a}
$$


# Gravitational Force

Mutual force of attraction between any two masses.
$$
F_{g}=G \frac{m_{1}m_{2}}{r^{2}}
$$

# Weight

The magnitude of the gravitational force acting on an object of mass near the Earth's surface. (not an inherent property)

# Newton's Third Law

If object 1 and object 2 interact, the force exerted by object 1 on object 2 is equal in magnitude but opposite in direction to the force exerted by object 2 on object 1.
$$
\vec{F}_{12}=-\vec{F}_{21}
$$

# External and Internal Forces

External force: Interaction between the object and its environment
Internal force: Within the object itself. ==Cannot change the object's velocity==.

# Applications of Newton's Laws

Conditions:
- Objects behave as particles
- Masses of strings or ropes are negligible
- Only interested in the forces acting on the object(neglect reaction forces)

# Equilibrium

An object at rest or moving with a constant velocity is said to be equilibrium.
The net force is zero.
$$
\sum \vec{F}=0
$$
$$
\sum F_{x}=0,\sum F_{y}=0
$$

# Forces of Friction

A force resistance to the relative motion, called friction.

For static friction, $f_{s}=F$
For Kinetic Friction, $f_{k}=\mu F_{n}$
