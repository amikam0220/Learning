# Kinematics

Focus on the ==description== of motion, not the ==cause== of the motion.

# Quantities for Motion

Three concepts:
- Displacement, Distance
- Velocity, Speed
- Acceleration

Position is defined in terms of a ==frame of reference==.
Displacement is defined as ==the change in position==.
Distance relates to the ==actual path traveled==.

# Vector and Scalar Quantities

Vector: both magnitude and direction
Scalar: only magnitude

# Speed and Velocity

Average speed is total distance divide by total time. 
$$
v = \frac{d}{t}
$$
where $v$ is a scalar quantity.

Average velocity is the ==rate== at which the displacement occurs.
$$
\vec{v} = \frac{\Delta \vec{x}}{\Delta t}
$$
direction will be the same as the direction of the displacement.

## Instantaneous Velocity

It is what happens at each ==point of time==, the tangent line at a point.
$$
\vec{v}=\lim_{ \Delta t \to 0 } \frac{\Delta \vec{x}}{\Delta t}=\frac{d\vec{x}}{dt}
$$

# Acceleration

Acceleration is ==the rate of change== of the velocity.
$$
\vec{a}=\frac{\Delta \vec{v}}{\Delta t}
$$
Instantaneous acceleration is the ==first time derivative of velocity==. 
$$
\vec{a}=\frac{d\vec{v}}{dt}=\frac{d^{2}\vec{x}}{dt^{2}}
$$
# Kinematic Equations

For ==uniform equations==:
$$
\begin{align}
 & v=v_{0}+at \\
 & \Delta x=\bar{v}t=\frac{1}{2}(v_{0}+v)t \\
 & \Delta x=v_{0}t+\frac{1}{2}at^{2} \\
 & v^{2}=v_{0}^{2}+2a\Delta x
\end{align}
$$

# Acceleration due to Gravity

Symbolized by $g$, always ==directed downward==. Free fall is constantly accelerated motion.
