# Forms of Energy

- Mechanical
- Chemical
- Electromagnetic
- Nuclear

# Work-Energy and Force

$$
W=F\cos\theta\Delta x
$$
Work is scalar quantity. 

## Kinetic Energy

$$
KE=\frac{1}{2}mv^{2}
$$
and 
$$
W_{net}=KE_{f}-KE_{i}=\Delta KE
$$
# Types of Forces

Two general classes of forces:
- conservative: Work and energy associated with the force are only related to initial and final position.
- Nonconservative: Related to path.

# Potential Energy

Associated with the position of the object within some system. It is property of the system, not the object.
For every conservative force, a potential energy function can be found.

# Work-Energy Theorem

$$
W_{nc}=(KE_{f}-KE_{i})+(PE_{f}-PE_{i})
$$

# Conservation of Energy

Total mechanical energy is the sum of the kinetic and potential energies in the system
$$
\begin{align}
 & E_{i}=E_{f} \\
 & KE_{i}+PE_{i}=KE_{f}+PE_{f}
\end{align}
$$
# Springs

Involves ==spring constant==, $k$. And Hooke's Law gives
$$
\vec{F}=-k\vec{x}
$$
The $F$ is the ==restoring force==, which is conservative force.
Thus it corresponds to ==elastic potential energy==. And 
$$
PE_{s}=\frac{1}{2}kx^{2}
$$
# Nonconservative Forces with Energy Considerations

When nonconservative force are present, the total ==mechanical energy== is ==not constant==. And the work done by all nonconservative forces equals to the change of mechanical energy:
$$
W_{nc}=\Delta E
$$
# Power-Energy Transfer

Power is the ==rate== where the force works on an object, defined as 
$$
P_{avg}=\frac{W}{\Delta t}
$$
And instantaneous power:
$$
P=\frac{dW}{dt}
$$
or 
$$
P=\vec{F}\cdot \vec{v}
$$
# Center of Mass

The ==point== in the body at which all the mass can be considered to be concentrated. The ==potential energy== is related to the position of center of mass.

For many particles in two-dimensional plane, the position vector 
$$
\vec{r_{i}}=x_{i}\vec{i}+y_{i}\vec{j}+z_{i}\vec{k}
$$
the position of the center of mass of a system of particles is: 
$$
\vec{r_{com}}=x_{com}\vec{i}+y_{com}\vec{j}+z_{com}\vec{k}
$$

for $x_{com}$,
$$
\vec{x_{com}}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{n} m_{i}x_{i}
$$
where $M=m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{n}$
Thus 
$$
\vec{r_{com}}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{n} m_{i}\vec{r_{i}}
$$

# Work Done by Varying Forces

Equal to the area under the graph of $F$ vs. $x$.
$$
W=\int_{x_{i}}^{x_{f}} F(x) \, dx 
$$
# Work-Kinetic Energy Theorem with a Variable Force

$$
W = \int_{v_{i}}^{v_{f}} mv \, dv=\frac{1}{2}mv_{f}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}mv_{i}^{2} 
$$
