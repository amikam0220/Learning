# Heat

The exchange of energy between objects because of temperature differences is called ==heat==.
Objects are in ==thermal contact== if energy can be exchanged between them.
==Thermal equilibrium== exists when two objects in thermal contact with each other ==cease to exchange energy==.

# Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics

If objects A and B are ==separately in thermal equilibrium== with a third object, then A and B are ==in thermal equilibrium== with each other.

This law allows the definition of temperature.

==Temperature== is the property that determines ==whether an object is in thermal equilibrium with other objects==.

# Absolute Zero

It is obtained by ==extrapolating== the law followed by ideal gases. When the temperature drops to $-273.15^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$, the ==volume or pressure== of the gas will decrease to zero.

Absolute zero has never been reached.

# Modern Definition of Kelvin Scale

It is defined in terms of ==two points==, one of which is ==absolute zero== and the second is the ==triple point of water==(single point where water can exist as solid, liquid and gas at the same time). It is $273.16K$.

# Superconductivity

The phenomenon that the electrical resistance of certain metals, alloys, and compounds suddenly decreases when temperature drops to ==near absolute zero==.

# Converting Among Temperature Scales

$$
\begin{align}
 & T_{c}=T_{K}-273.15 \\
 & T_{F}=\frac{9}{5}T_{c}+32 \\
 & T_{C}=\frac{5}{9}(T_{F}-32) \\
 & \Delta T_{F}=\frac{9}{5}\Delta T_{C}
\end{align}
$$
# Thermal Expansion

Thermal expansion is the consequence of the change in ==average separation== between its constituent atoms or molecules. As temperature increases, the ==vibrating amplitude increases==. And it causes the overall object to expand.

# Structure of Graphite

The structure ==between layers== exhibits the characteristics of ==atomic crystal==, and the structure ==in the layer== exhibits the characteristics of ==molecular crystal==.

# Linear Expansion

For small change in temperature,
$$
\Delta L=\alpha L_{0}\Delta T
$$
where $\alpha$ is the ==coefficient of linear expansion==, depends on the material(average coefficients).

# Area Expansion

Two dimensions expand according to 
$$
\Delta A=\gamma A_{0}\Delta t,\gamma=2\alpha
$$
where $\gamma$ is the coefficient of area expansion.

![[area expansion.png]]

# Volume Expansion

Three dimensions expand
$$
\Delta V=\beta V_{0}\Delta t
$$
for solids, $\beta=3\alpha$.

# Unusual Behavior of Water

When temperature of water increases from $0$ to $4^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$, its ==density== ==increases==.

Maximum density of water is $1000kg /m^{3}$ at $4^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$.

# Heat

The transfer of ==energy== between a system and its environment because of ==temperature difference==.
And $Q$ is used to represent the amount.

The unit is $\mathrm{cal}$. A calorie is the amount of energy to raise the temperature of $1g$ water from $14.5$ to $15.5$ $^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$. And $1\mathrm{cal}=4.186J$, which is called the ==mechanical equivalent of heat==.

# Specific Heat

The specific heat, $c$, is the requirement of amount of ==energy per unit mass== to change ==the temperature== of that substance by $1^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$.

$$
c=\frac{Q}{m\Delta T}
$$

where $\Delta T$ is still ==the final temperature minus the initial temperature==. If $\Delta T$ is positive then $\Delta Q$ is also positive. The energy flows into the system. And vice versa.

# Calorimetry

Calorimetry is the analysis performed using a ==calorimeter==, which can reach thermal equilibrium without loss energy to environment.

==Conservation of energy== in a isolated system:
$$
Q_{\mathrm{cold}}=-Q_{\mathrm{hot}}
$$

## Calorimeter

A device to measure the ==calorific value== of solids and liquids.
The ==heat capacity== is the ==amount of heat absorbed per $1^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$==.
$$
C=\frac{Q}{\Delta T}
$$

# Phase Changes

When the physical characteristics of the substance change from one to another such as solid to liquid.

Phases changes involve a change in the ==internal energy== but ==no change in temperature==.

# Latent Heat

The absorbed or emitted heat during a phase change.
$$
Q=\pm mL
$$
where $L$ is the ==latent heat== of the substance, which depends on ==substance== and ==nature of phase change==.

Latent heat of fusion $L_{f}$ is used for melting or freezing. Latent heat of vaporization $L_{v}$ is used for boiling or condensing.

# Sublimation

The process that some substance go directly ==from solid to gas== without passing liquid phase. Latent heat of sublimation is associated with the change.

## Affection Factors

- Intermolecular Forces: 
	Interactions weaken, allowing molecules to gain energy to directly from solid to gas.
- Thermodynamic Equilibrium:
	The chemical potential difference between the solid and gas phases is not sufficiently large.
- Kinetic Factors:
	The diffusion rate of molecules and the rate of surface reconstruction
