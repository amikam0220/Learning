# Some Properties of Nuclei

- All nuclei are composed of ==protons and neutrons==(exception ordinary hydrogen)
- ==Atomic number== $Z$ equals the ==number of protons==  in the nucleus
- ==Neutron number== $N$ equals the number of ==neutrons== in the nucleus
- Mass number $A$ is the ==number of nucleons== in the nucleus
	$A=Z+N$

Symbol
$$
_{Z}^{A}X
$$
where $X$ is the chemical symbol of the element.

Same element may contain ==varying numbers of neutrons==. They are called the ==isotopes==.

# Charge and Mass

Proton: $+e$; Electron: $-e$; Neutron has no charge

Define the mass of one atom of C-12 is $12u$, where $u$ is called the ==unified mass unit==.

# Size of Nucleus

Investigated by Rutherford in scatting experiments
Most nuclei are approximately spherical. Average radius is $r=r_{0} A^{1 / 3}$.(femtometers, fm)
Density: all nuclei have nearly the same density. The ==volume== of the nucleus is proportional to the ==total number== of nucleons.

# Nuclear Stability

The nuclei are stable due to the short-range force called the ==nuclear force== to attract, which is much stronger than electrostatic force.

If stable:
Light nuclei: N = Z
Heavy nuclei: N > Z
No nuclei: Z>83

# Binding Energy

The ==total energy== of the ==bound system== (the nucleus) is ==less than== the ==combined energy== of the separated nucleons. The ==difference== is binding energy.

Binding Energy Per nucleon:
Except for light nuclear, the binding energy per nucleon is stable when A > 40. Small for light nuclei and large for mid nuclei (peak when A = 60)

# Radioactivity

The ==spontaneous== emission of radiation. The result of ==decay or disintegration of unstable nuclei==.

Three types:
- Alpha Particles
	$^{4}\mathrm{He}$
- Beta Particle
	electron or positrons
- Gamma rays
	high energy photons


| Type               | Composition                | Charge  | Mass       | Penetrating Ability | Ionizing Ability |
| ------------------ | -------------------------- | ------- | ---------- | ------------------- | ---------------- |
| $\alpha$ particles | $^{4}\mathrm{He}$($2p+2n$) | $+2e$   | $4u$       | low                 | high             |
| $\beta$ particles  | electrons or positrons     | $\pm e$ | $0.00055u$ | mid                 | mid              |
| $\gamma$ rays      | photons                    | mid     | 0          | high                | low              |

To distinguish them, 
- Magnetic field: gamma no deflection, alpha deflected opposite to beta. (If positive is same but the radius of alpha is larger)
- Penetrating experiment
- Ionizing experiment

# The Decay Constant

The number of particles that decay in a given time is ==proportional== to the ==total number== of particles.
$$
\Delta N=-\lambda N\Delta t
$$
where $\lambda$ is called the decay constant.
The decay rate:
$$
R=\left| \frac{\Delta N}{\Delta t} \right|=\lambda N 
$$
Unit: $\mathrm{Curie}, \mathrm{Ci} , 1 \mathrm{Ci}=3.7\times10^{10} decays/second$. $\mathrm{Bq},1\mathrm{Bq}=1 decays / second$
The decay curve:
$$
N=N_{0}e^{-\lambda t}
$$
And the half-life is the time takes for half of nuclei to decay
$$
T_{1 / 2}= \frac{\ln2}{\lambda}
$$

# Decay

The process of one element changing into another element is called ==spontaneous decay== or ==transmutation==.
The sum of mass number and atomic number are the same on both side of the equation.
Conservation of mass-energy and momentum hold

## Alpha Decay

$$
^{A}_{Z}\mathrm{X}\to_{Z-2}^{A-4}\mathrm{Y}+_{2}^{4}\mathrm{He}
$$
The mass of parent nucleus is ==larger== than the total mass of He and daughter nucleus. Extra mass are convert into Kinetic energy of particles($E=\Delta mc^{2}$)

## Beta Decay

$$
\begin{align}
 & ^{A}_{Z}\mathrm{X}\to_{Z+1}^{A}\mathrm{Y}+e^{-}+\hat{v} \\
 & ^{A}_{Z}\mathrm{X}\to_{Z-1}^{A}\mathrm{Y}+e^{+}+v \\
\end{align}
$$
$v$ is the symbol of neutrino and $\hat{v}$ is antineutrino. (To satisfy the mass conservation)

## Gamma Decay

Excited state to base state
$$
^{12}_{6}C^{*}\to ^{12}_{6}C+\gamma
$$
The gamma emission does not change element.

# Natural Radioactivity

Unstable nuclei found in nature gives rise to natural radioactivity(Uranium, Actinium, Thorium). Produced in lab through nuclear reactions exhibit artificial radioactivity.

# Nuclear Reactions

Bombarding nuclei with energetic particles causes the change of structure of nuclei. This is called ==nuclear reactions==.
Radiation absorbed by matter can cause damage.
# Q Values and Threshold Energy

The energy required to balance a nuclear reaction is ==$Q$ value==. (Positive for endothermic reaction, negative for exothermic reaction)

The minimum amount of kinetic energy to conserve the momentum and energy is called the ==threshold energy==.
$$
KE_{min}=\left( 1+ \frac{m}{M} \right)\left| Q \right| 
$$
