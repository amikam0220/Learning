# Process of Nuclear Energy

- Fission
	Large mass number nucleus ==splits== into two smaller nuclei
- Fusion
	Two light nuclei ==fuse== to form a heavier nucleus

# Nuclear Fission

A heavy nucleus ==splits== into two smaller nuclei

$$
_{0}^{1}\mathrm{n}+_{92}^{235}\mathrm{U}\to_{92}^{236}\mathrm{U}^{*}\to \mathrm{X}+\mathrm{Y}+n\;\mathrm{neutrons}
$$
mid element is intermediate. $X$ and $Y$ are called fission fragments.
The fission fragments have less mass than original nuclei. This decrease in mass appears ad released energy.
This fission releases much larger energy than chemical processes.

# Chain Reaction

The neutrons emitted by fission ==triggers fission of other nucleus==. This process is called a ==chain reaction==.
If uncontrolled, a violent explosion.
If controlled, nuclear reactor

# Nuclear Reactor

A system designed to maintain a ==self-sustained== chain reaction.
The reproduction constant $k$ is defined as the average number of neutrons from each fission that cause another fission ($k=1$, critical; $k<1$, subcritical; $k>1$, supercritical)

## Design

- Fuel elements: Enriched uranium
- Moderator: slow down the neutrons
- Control rods: absorb neutrons

# Pressurized Water Reactor

Maintain water in high pressure to keep it from boiling.
- hot water in primary system pumped through a heat exchanger
- Heat transferred to the water in secondary system
- water converted into steam
- Steam create electric power
- water in secondary system isolated from in primary system to prevent pollution

# Reactor Safety

- Three level of containment: vessel, building, location
- Emergency cooling system
- Disposal of waste material
- Transportation of fuel and waste

# Nuclear Fusion

Two light nuclei combine to form a heavier nucleus.

The mass of final nucleus is less than the masses of the original nuclei.

# Fusion in the Sun

Conditions
- High temperature
- High rate of collisions

# Proton-Proton Cycle

A series of three nuclear reactions believed to operate in the Sun. Energy liberated is ==gamma rays, positrons and neutrinos==.

# Fusion Reactors

Advantages
- Inexpensive fuel source
- few by-products

Considerations:
- High temperature
- Overcome Coulomb repulsion

# Requirements for Successful Thermonuclear Reactor

Plasma ion density $n$; Plasma confinement time $\tau$
Lawson's Criteria
- $n\tau\geq 10^{14} s/cm^{3}$ for deuterium-tritium
- $n\tau>10^{16} s / cm^{3}$ for deuterium-deuterium

# Methods of Creating Fusion Events

- Magnetic confinement
- Inertial laser confinement
- Inertial electrostatic confinement
