# Unit 4 How to Make Refutations

## What is a Refutation

A refutation attempts to point out problems with opposing view and to show where an opponent's argument breaks down.

- the counterclaim
- our position
- our refutation

## Why to refute

- To get readers to listen to our perspective.
- To make ourselves more believable.
- To understand our own arguments better.

## How to refute

- Point out the counterargument's limits by putting the opposing view in a larger context.
- Tell the other side of the story.
- Address logical fallacies in the counterargument.

## How to signal a refutation

### Thinking of the opposing position

It (has been/may be/could be)
- argued/asserted/claimed/said that
- contended/maintained that

# Unit 8 How to Write with cohesion and coherence

## What is cohesion and coherence

cohesion:
	the degree to which sentences or paragraphs are connected so that the flow of ideas is easy to follow(==at the language level==)
coherence:
	the overall understandability of what we write or say.(==at the logic level==)

## For Cohesion

### Grammatical

- substitution:

- ellipsis

- conjunction
