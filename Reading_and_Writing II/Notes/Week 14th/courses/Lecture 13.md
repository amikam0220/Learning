# Unit 5 How to Develop Effective Reasoning

## What is Reasoning

### Logos

Appeal to the audience's capacity for ==logical reasoning==.

### Ethos

==Establish the author's credibility== with the audience.
#### Strategy

- Draw attention directly to your ==credentials==.
- Draw a portrait of ==your personality and character==.

### Pathos

Use ==emotional appeal== to persuade the audience.

## Modes of Reasoning

### Deduction

The reasoning process which takes place when moving from one statement (==the major premise==) through another (==the minor premise==) to yet another statement (==the conclusion==) is called deduction. 

- Make a general claim
- Apply it to an smaller example or case
- Draw a conclusion

### Induction

Induction uses information about observed cases (==specific facts==) to reach a conclusion about unobserved cases (==general facts==).

- Present some examples
- Draw a conclusion/claim based on them

# Unit 6 How to Avoid Logical Fallacies(==No== exam)

## What Are Logical Fallacies

Logical fallacies are logically unsound statement.

## Types of Logical Fallacies

### Fallacies of relevance

The lack of a relevant logical connection between premise and conclusion.
#### Personal attack

Attack ==the person rather than the argument== itself.

#### Argument to the people

Use an appeal to ==popular assent== by arousing the feelings and enthusiasm of multitude rather than building an argument.

#### Appeal to tradition

Assert a premise just because people ==always believed or done it==.

#### False authority

Build the argument just by ==an authority figure believes or does something== even if it is not expertise.

### Fallacies of defective or week induction

A lack of understanding of the required logical link between premise and conclusion.

#### Post hoc, ergo propter hoc

One event causes another just because ==they occurred in sequence==.

#### Hasty Generalization

Induct with ==insufficient evidence==.

#### Non sequitur

Argument does ==not follow from the previous statement==.

### Fallacies of presumption

Unstated or shaky presumption is made.

#### Self-contradiction

Argument is ==contradict== with premises.

#### Circular reasoning

State a argument by ==restating it in different words== as evidence.

#### The "slippery slope" fallacy

Argue that once the ==first step is taken==, a second or third step will ==inevitably follow==.

#### Either/Or fallacy

Reason that there are ==only two possibility== when there may be many.

#### Faulty analogy

Presume that if two things or people are ==alike in some ways== then they will ==be alike in other ways==.

### Fallacies of ambiguity(less in exam)

Ambiguous language.
